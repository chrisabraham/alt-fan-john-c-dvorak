Why I did this, and who the fuck I am

The heartfelt appeal behind alt.fan.john-c-dvorak.

The charter and the paperwork live on the main page. This page is the reason.

Who the fuck I am

My name is Chris Abraham. I live in Arlington, Virginia, and I am, in the matter at hand, exactly one thing: a listener. I found Adam Curry's Daily Source Code in 2005, when I had just moved to Berlin to live there, in a flat at Lehrterstrasse 5d in Tiergarten. I heard Adam lose his mother on that podcast. I heard him piss in his garage, because the show stopped for nothing. In 2007 Adam and a tech journalist named John C. Dvorak started No Agenda, and I was there for episode one. I stayed for all of it.

Somewhere along the way the show dubbed me a Knight of the No Agenda order. I don't know that I ever achieved a title above Knight, and that is all I ever needed. I never even ordered the ring.

By trade I do search work. By disposition I am a reader, a Mason, and a man who keeps his receipts: the address on this page, cja@well.com, is one I have had since 1996. I proposed this group, I wrote its charter, and I maintain this site. That is the whole of my authority here. Nobody appointed me. A newsgroup does not require appointment. It requires a proponent, and I am him.

Why I care

Because for nineteen years, through all of Adam's homes and wives and beliefs, through every era of that show and every era of my own life on two continents, John C. Dvorak was the constant. The anchor. The dad. The husband. The stalwart. Adam was the weather; John was the climate. You built your week around both of them, Thursdays and Sundays, and the voice doing the grounding, the correcting, the refusing-to-be-impressed, was John's.

John died on July 20, 2026. I did not know how much I would miss him, and I am still mourning. You are not supposed to grieve a voice in your earbuds the way you grieve family, except that nineteen years is its own kind of family, and grief does not ask permission. I got back onto Usenet this year out of nostalgia, and I found the alt.fan hierarchy full of the writers and broadcasters of John's era, and his name missing from it. That is the whole origin of this group: an absence I could do something about.

Why Usenet

Because a memorial to John C. Dvorak should not have a landlord. Everything else we build for the dead now sits on a platform that can sunset it, demonetize it, bury it under an algorithm, or sell the building out from under it. A Usenet newsgroup is the opposite arrangement. Nobody controls it, nobody owns it, and nobody will really find it unless they come looking, which is exactly the congregation a memorial wants. Yes, there will be haters. There were haters in every comment section John ever had, and he outlived them all by the simple method of continuing. The group is unmoderated, in plain text, on servers run by strangers who peer with each other out of conviction. It is the medium of the era John helped define, and it will hold his name in the hierarchy for as long as Usenet runs, which so far is forty-five years and counting.

It is long, long, long, long overdue, and posthumous, which is the worst kind of overdue there is. I could not do anything about the timing. I could do something about the absence.

The appeal

If John taught you something, argued you out of something, or kept you company across a commute or an ocean, come and say so in the group. Post one memory. Post the column that changed your mind, or the prediction that came embarrassingly true. If you disagreed with him every week for nineteen years, you are especially welcome; he would have insisted on it. A newsgroup stays alive exactly as long as people talk in it, and this one's subject spent his career starting arguments worth having.

Olly olly oxen free. Come out, come out.
